It is held at its post spacers, F.Cu lips, rail blocks and USB-C housings, but nothing props its middle, so the middle keeps going after the cup stops. Colour is the surface strain; MLCCs and solder joints crack in drop tests from about 1,000 to 2,000 µε.

What you're seeing

  • Board face: the cup stops first. The board slides 0.4 mm onto its post spacers, its middle carries on toward the wall (up in the Iso view), and F.Cu parts come within about 0.06 mm of it. Then it springs back toward the cells.
  • Back face: the cans move away from the board and the rail blocks pull it along at four corners. The unsupported middle sags toward the cells by up to 2 mm and rings.
  • The strain peaks at the USB-C receptacles' tabs and around the spacers, which are point supports. The 99th-percentile number in the panel leaves those spikes out.

How to read it

  • Depth across the board (wall to cells) is stretched by the Bend slider, measured from each board face, so contacts still read true. The board's own 1.6 mm thickness is drawn true. The surface colour is lightly smoothed; the numbers are not. The cans keep their true round shape, so only their crowns sit at true distance.
  • Linear plate model with 60 modes, 2 % damping, driven by the cup's motion from the contact drop model. It is a guide to where the board bends, not a crack predictor.
