The lap has to sit inside the 14 mm weld window, because the gap under the frame only fits one 0.2 mm layer. That means a 0.2 mm joggle in the copper, and weld spots kept at least 3 mm from the solder.

No wire fits the 0.25 mm gap under the frame, so the frame has to be notched right through for each lead. The hole centre is pushed up to 5.75 mm so its ring clears the board edge. Moving the solder joint out to the cell edge would avoid the notches, but it would leave under 1 mm of wire before the board, so you'd lose the flex that makes wire worth having.

Option A routed: all seven connections

Plain nickel

Each cell junction reaches the gauge through a thin nickel tail. The tails carry only sense and balancing current (milliamps), so their size is set by handling, not resistance. The two PACK tabs carry the load current; their narrow Kelvin fingers are the gauge's sense points for the pack's two ends.
