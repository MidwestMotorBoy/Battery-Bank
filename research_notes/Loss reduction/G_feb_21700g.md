## What else it changes
- **Charging** must be capped at ~4.55 A per cell (~68 W in) and lower when cool (3.25 A below 15 °C, 0.65 A below 5 °C): BQ25756 charge current and JEITA settings.
- **Discharge**: firmware must hold the battery current to 6.5 A once cells pass ~35 °C and 3.25 A past ~45 °C (if the derating is by cell temperature; if it's ambient, it bites on hot days).
- **Short circuit is easier**: higher resistance means ~150–170 A, below today's ~200 A.
- **Fit**: up to 21.85 × 71.15 mm. The cell pitch, cup bore, frame windows and webs need ~0.4 mm more; the pack grows ~0.7 mm across and ~0.4 mm long.
- **Gauge**: a new chemistry ID (likely a silicon-blend anode energy cell).
- **Brand**: FEB is obscure; quality and consistency are unproven without the lab data.

## Verdict
The best cell for energy (+14 % over the 58E, +8 % over the 60XG, cheapest per Wh, longest at 30 W) and the worst for heat: 100 W becomes a ~6 minute burst, and full charging takes ~2 h. Good if the bank lives at phone and tablet loads and 100 W is rare; the 60XG is the choice if fast bursts and charging matter.
